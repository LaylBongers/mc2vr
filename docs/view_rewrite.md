# View rewrite (the `viewContextData` channel)

Code: `src/carrier/vr/view_rewrite.cpp`. The visible view lives in the VS constant `viewContextData` (layout: VP rows 0..3, optional camPos, optional world-fixed extra row, row-major, `clip_i = dot(VP_row_i, worldpos)`; the count-6 extra row is left alone — RE: [view_context_records.md](reverse_engineering/view_context_records.md)).

## The per-eye view record

`SubmitToGPU` (reimplemented) uploads the record (the record's `g_ViewContextTable` entry) up to three times: VS `viewContextData`, PS `viewContextData` (`depthXform` in the depth prepass) and PS `cameraPos` (`+0x40`). Every upload starts at the record's base, so the rewrite works on the record itself and needs no register map. `view_rewrite::eye_view_record` makes this pass's eye copy: VP rows 0..3 rebuilt, camPos = the eye position, the count-6 row and the rest copied. It is made once per record and pass (a per-pass cache keyed by the record address, made again if the game's first 0x60 bytes change), and all three uploads read it. The main-pass gate ([Pass gate](#pass-gate)) is checked per record, after `RenderShell_SetViewType` has bound its targets. The HMD stamp lookup and the no-pose pass-through happen when the copy is made. A VS upload of an eye record makes its view the one the [explicit camera matrices](#explicit-camera-matrices) are corrected against. The device's `SetVertexShaderConstantF` tap carries only the explicit matrices.

Log, per window: `view/hmd: view records produced eye=… offscreen=… notHmd=… noPose=… failed=…` (per record and pass) and `view/hmd: view record uploads: VS viewContextData … | PS viewContextData … | PS cameraPos …` (per upload, same sources). Expected: on the main pass, PS uploads are `eye`; they pass through as `offscreen`, and as `noPose` without an HMD. Every main-pass record's copy is made, uploaded or not, so productions also count records that never upload a view block: about one `failed` per pass (an all-zero VP, no camera) and two `notHmd`.

Live (2026-11-12, gameplay, 87–90 Hz): every PS `viewContextData` and `cameraPos` upload `eye`; VS `eye` plus `offscreen` (the shadow atlas); no `notHmd`/`failed` uploads; the explicit matrices `noDelta=0`, both shadow-box checks `bad=0`. No visible regressions.

## Modes

- `hmd_delta` (always on): per-eye OpenXR-FOV projection + per-eye position/rotation delta relative to the union pose the uploaded VP carries ([HMD view stamps](#hmd-view-stamps)), applied to the main pass's view records ([above](#the-per-eye-view-record)) via `vp_camera::decompose` + `apply_eye`. Without a tracked HMD the uploads pass through.
- `debug_view_identity=on` (oracle, formerly `hmd_identity`): rebuilds the game's own camera and logs the max residual — the one-shot consistency check; also the calibration input for the camtable row-sign measurement.

Historical verification modes — `on`/`pulse` (row-shift pans), `stereo` (±right·IPD/2 A/B with the right-axis cache, `view_asym` NDC shift, `view_stereo_hold`), `hmd` (full-VP replacement) — were REMOVED 2026-10-07, superseded by the union injection + `hmd_delta` (git history has them). The math below survives as the machinery `hmd_delta` runs on.

## Math

- World-space pan `D = (dx,dy,dz)`: camPos `xyz += D`, and every VP row `w -= dot(row.xyz, D)` (rigid world shift on screen; a uniform clip-w shift does NOT work — the divide scales it per-vertex). The camera right axis is `normalize(row0.xyz)` (view row0 = camera right; refresh on main-pass row0 uploads only — offscreen passes must not seed it).
- Full VP decomposition/rebuild: `clip = [a·x_v + c·z_v, b·y_v + d·z_v, A·z_v + B, z_v]` with `x_v/y_v/z_v = dot(R/U/F, p−C)`. So rows are `row0 = aR + cF`, `row1 = bU + dF`, `row2 = A·F`, `row3 = F` (xyz), with `w = −dot(xyz, C)` (row2.w additionally `+B`). Decompose: `F = row3.xyz` (must be unit — else pass the technique through and count it), `R,a` from row0 minus its F component, `U,b` likewise, `c,d` the F components, `A = row2·F` (row2 must be ∥ F), `C` from solving `clip.x=clip.y=clip.w=0` (3×3 Cramer, independent of the optional camPos row), `B = row2.w + row2·C`. Pure math: `src/carrier/vr/vp_camera.{hpp,cpp}` on `src/carrier/vr/vec_math.hpp`; native test `tools/test/test_vp_camera.cpp` covers decompose/rebuild round trip, identity eye, eye-shift/head-turn directions, scale, `clip_delta` and `view_delta`.

## HMD view stamps

When the view builder finishes an HMD-driven view ([culling.md](culling.md): identified by its camera's source), it stamps that view's VP with the union pose and HMD sample of the camera-table entry its camera came from (`view_rewrite::on_hmd_view_built`, an 8-entry seqlocked ring: two HMD views per frame, a few frames back). Every record the main pass uploads is an exact copy of a view's VP, made by several writers (the builder's own record, `Scene_EmitViewPrimitives`' copies), and `viewContextData` carries its transpose. So a record is looked up (once per pass) by exact equality with a stamp's transposed VP: the pose the delta is taken against is the one that VP was built with, whichever writer made the record and however late it is uploaded. A record without a stamp isn't an HMD view and passes through. `view/hmd: HMD views hit=… notHmd=…` counts both per window; the camera table returns the newest rewrite of an entry address (the same entries are rewritten every frame or two).

## Pose age

The frame is built (view, culling, records) with the HMD sample the camera-table fill took; its eye passes render with the OpenXR frame's newer views. The build-time sample is 2 OpenXR frames older (~22 ms at 90 Hz); the head turns 0.2–1.5° on average between the two, up to ~15° in fast turns (`view/hmd: poseAge`, once per frame). `hmd_delta`'s per-pass eye delta absorbs that rotation: per-eye camera data produced at build time needs the same late, rotation-only correction per pass, or it adds that latency.

## Explicit camera matrices

Shaders without `viewContextData` take the camera as `LocalToProj` (CTAB names, `vr/shader_constants`). It gets `D·M`, where `D` maps this pass's mono clip space to the eye's (`D·VP_mono = VP_eye`). `D` is built from the two cameras' parameters (`vp_camera::clip_delta`: `P_eye · T · P_mono⁻¹`, with `T` the eye's rigid offset from the mono camera in camera space and `P⁻¹` in closed form), not by inverting the world-space VP: its ~1e3-unit translations would round into every corrected matrix. An identity eye gives `D = I` to the axes' float rounding, wherever the camera is (`tools/test/test_vp_camera.cpp`).

**View-space matrices** (`LocalToView` = view · world) get `T·M` instead: `T` is `vp_camera::view_delta`, the same factor `D` is built around, so `T·View_mono·World = View_eye·World` without large numbers. The game's view frame and `vp_camera`'s have opposite handedness: the game's view x points to screen-left (y up, z forward), `vp_camera`'s to screen-right, the D3D left-handed convention its clip rows decompose into. A single negated axis converts between them: `LocalToView = S·View·World` with `S = diag(−1, 1, 1, 1)`, so `T` is applied as `S·T·S`. The camera table's `S_r` is the same change of handedness ([camera.md](camera.md)), and the game's projection's negative x scale (`LocalToProj` row 0 = −a · `LocalToView` row 0) is what takes its frame into D3D's clip space. `vp_camera` never sees it: `decompose` reads R as screen-right straight from the clip rows. So `S` is needed only where the game's own view-frame matrices are used: `LocalToView`, `ViewToLocal` and the camera-table entries. `P_mono·S·LocalToView = LocalToProj` holds on every record (relative residuals ≤ 1e-3; with `S = I` they are 0.8–2), and each record checks this on its mono values (`LocalToProj` is uploaded first; bad = residual > 5e-3, where a sign error gives ~1). The pixel shader's `ViewToLocal` (the inverse of `LocalToView`) gets `M·(S·T·S)⁻¹`. Both are logged per window as `view/hmd: shadow boxes: LocalToView rows=… ViewToLocal rows=… | checks: convention … inverse … | noL2P=… split=… noReg=…`.

The clouds' sky matrices (`g_ViewProjMtx`, used by no other shader) are the exception: they are produced per eye at their emitters ([Clouds](#clouds)), and the device tap never touches `g_ViewProjMtx`.

Rewrites are done on **copies** (the eye record; a scratch copy returned to the driver call for the matrices): the game's records and upload buffers are never modified. `view_world_scale` scales the HMD position offset (units are metres, s = 1.0 — [camera.md](camera.md)).

## Clouds

`PgClouds_EmitCloudRender1` and `PgClouds_EmitCloudRender2` (reimplemented: `reimpl/render/clouds_emit.cpp`) each build a constant block (cloud shape, colours, ranges: no camera terms; the camera translation enters multiplied by zero) and a camera-relative sky matrix `transpose(inverse(camera rotation) · P)`: rows 0, 1 and 3 have w = 0, `P` is the view's projection from the ctx tans with the sky's own depth terms (`A'` = 1.0000019, `B'` = −`A'`). They draw with the shader pairs `Pg_RegisterShaders` registers as `PgCloudRenderVP1`/`FP1` and `PgCloudRenderVP2`/`FP2`. Before both, `PgClouds_EmitCloudGen` (not reimplemented) renders the cloud texture into a 512×512 target (`PgCloudGenFP1`: four offset noise channels, weighted, then clamped and scaled). It has no camera terms, so both eye passes redraw the same image; that is harmless. Draw order in each eye pass (live, `debug_gui_census=clouds`): generation, Render1, Render2. Render 1 (tech 84, `g_ViewProjMtx` c12 + `viewContextData`) goes into the half-size `mCloudOutRT` (the stream binds it after its constant uploads); render 2 (tech 85) goes into main with scene depth (LESSEQUAL, no z-write; its PS fades by the stored depth).

The records are built once per frame and replayed in both passes, so the emitter registers each sky matrix (`view_rewrite::on_sky_matrix_built`) with the view it was built for. The lookup goes through the view's HMD stamp ([HMD view stamps](#hmd-view-stamps)): the ctx's VP is matched exactly. When the record's stream uploads it (`RenderCmd_ExecuteStream` op 2, `on_record_vs_constant`), it is replaced by this pass's eye version. That version is the matrix decomposed (`C` = 0), with `apply_eye`'s rotation (union → this frame's eye pose, so the late correction of [Pose age](#pose-age) is included) and the eye's projection (its FOV tangents in place of the ctx tans), `A'`/`B'` kept, and no translation. This does not use `D·M`: `D` maps the main view's clip space, so `D·M` takes the main view's depth slope (`A` ≈ 1.0004) and cloud render 2's distant geometry (out to `cQuad3.x` = 7000) lands past the far plane, clipped. A matrix without a stamp or pose passes through mono, and so does a `g_ViewProjMtx` upload no emitter registered (counted as `unregistered`; never `D·M`). The gate is the stamp, not the RT0 size.

Log per window: `view/hmd: sky matrices (clouds): built hmd=… notHmd=… failed=… axesVsView max=… | uploads eye=… notHmd=… noPose=… unregistered=… identityResidMax=…`. `axesVsView` is the angle between the matrix's axes and its view's, which checks that the emitter's camera argument is the view's camera. It is measured as `2·asin(|a − b|/2)`: `acos` of a float dot product can only read multiples of about 0.02° near zero (0.0198°·√k for k ulps), and the render threads' x87 runs at 24 bits ([fpu_precision.hpp](../src/carrier/vr/fpu_precision.hpp)), so doubles would not help. Expected: 2 built per frame, all `hmd`; `axesVsView` ≈ 0; uploads `eye` ≈ 4 per frame (2 records × 2 passes), `unregistered=0`; `debug_view_identity=on` gives a residual ~1e-6. `debug_gui_census=clouds` shows the records' constants per eye pass (the reimplementation files its Submits under the originals' return addresses).

Live (2026-11-13, gameplay, 90 Hz, head turns up to 4.4° per 10 s window): 2 built and 4 `eye` uploads per frame, all `hmd`; `notHmd` only around loading; `axesVsView` = 0 (the emitters' camera argument is the view's camera); no other check regressed. Sky and clouds stable.

## Pass gate

Shadow-map, reflection and other offscreen passes upload their own `viewContextData` (the shadow pass's "camera" is the light); shifting them moved shadow maps relative to receivers (visible shadow fading). The rewrite applies only while RT0 (observed via device SetRenderTarget, slot 37) has the engine's LIVE render size (RenderShell `+0x2b98/+0x2b9c` = `g_ScreenWidth/Height`, read per check). (A size cached once goes stale after a resolution change and silently stops all rewriting — `rewritten rows=0`.) Seen RT0 sizes: 2560x1440 main; skipped: 1024x4096 shadow atlas, 512², 128², 64², 853x480, and the 1280x720 → 1x1 downsample chain. Caveat: an offscreen pass with exactly the render size would be shifted (none seen). Keying on RT identity (`g_RT_MainColorSurf` + the composite front) is the stricter alternative. Shadow receivers look up in world space (the VS passes world position to the PS) — eye-invariant.

Still mono: the camera-inside-box test that picks the render state (it switches only within about one box size of the camera). - **`depthXform` c0-3 ← the pass's `viewContextData`** (the view's `g_ViewContextTable` record; per eye, [the per-eye view record](#the-per-eye-view-record); the analysis below is why). The depth prepass into DepthBufferCopy ([msaa.md](msaa.md)): `shader3.bin 0x211020` (opaque, the bulk of the prepass draws), `0x2112f0` (alpha-tested), `0x2114b0` (alpha-tested + material); and `0x2721b0` (`depthMap` + `depthXform`: writes its own depth where it is in front of the stored one, else `texkill`; same draw count as the water PS, so presumably the water's depth pass). From the mono record, the depth *value* stored for every post and decal consumer would be measured along the mono forward axis, while the hardware depth is per eye. Rows 2–3 are `A·F, B` and `F, −F·C`: the eye offset is ⊥ F, and the per-eye projection keeps A/B (`vp_camera`), so the error is the union→eye rotation: the pose-age correction (0.2–1.5° on average, up to ~15°, [Pose age](#pose-age)) plus any display canting. It is the same record as the VS main view block, so it is produced per eye together with it. Per eye, the visible effect is slightly steadier shadow boxes, cloud edges and water in fast head turns.

From the mono record it is off by the eye offset (±IPD/2 ≈ 32 mm), losing the per-eye specular/fresnel difference, visible on close glossy surfaces and water. Same record again.
